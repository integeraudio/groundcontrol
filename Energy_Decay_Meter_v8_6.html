Before you start — three things to check:
1. Turn on your studio monitors or subwoofer and connect them as the default audio output.
2. Place a measurement mic (e.g. UMIK-1) at your mix position at ear level.
3. Keep the room quiet during the sweep — no fans, no HVAC if possible. Your browser will request microphone permission when you click Connect & Calibrate.
⚠ Measurement Limitations:
• Below 40 Hz — T60 values are filter-limited (1/3-octave filter ringing affects accuracy).
• Single position — Results are position-dependent. ISO 3382 recommends spatial averaging.
• Multi-sweep averaging — Each run averages 4 sweeps for better low-frequency SNR. Still best used for relative before/after comparison at a fixed mic/sub position.
• Subwoofer contribution — Low-frequency decay includes speaker ringing, not just room modes.

About This Test

This report measures how long bass energy lingers in the room after a tone stops, across the 20–80 Hz range. Four 15-second logarithmic sine sweeps were played through the studio monitors while a measurement microphone recorded the room's response; the recordings were pip-aligned and averaged together to improve low-frequency signal-to-noise. The averaged recording was deconvolved against the known sweep to produce the room impulse response, then Schroeder backward integration — with Lundeby noise truncation so the decay is measured only above the noise floor — was applied in 1/3-octave bands. The headline figure is the directly measured energy remaining a fixed time after the tone stops; T60 is reported as supporting detail. The professional monitoring target for T60 is 0.25–0.55 seconds.

Limitations: Single mic position; 4-sweep synchronous averaging; results are valid for relative before/after comparison at a fixed mic and sub position, not as absolute room values (ISO 3382 expects spatial averaging for those). Values below 40 Hz are filter-limited (the 1/3-octave filter's own ringing contributes). Bands with under 20 dB of clean decay above the noise floor are marked noise-limited and given no T60. Each band uses the same analysis method (T30/T20/T10) for both the before and after runs.
