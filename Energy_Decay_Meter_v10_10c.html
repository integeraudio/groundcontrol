Before you start — three things to check:
1. Turn on your studio monitors or subwoofer and connect them as the default audio output.
2. Place a measurement mic (e.g. UMIK-1) at your mix position at ear level.
3. Keep the room quiet during the sweep — no fans, no HVAC if possible. Your browser will request microphone permission when you click Connect & Calibrate.
⚠ Measurement Limitations:
• Below ~24 Hz — At the bottom edge of the 20–80 Hz sweep, energy and resolution are limited, so values there are less reliable.
• Single position — Results are position-dependent. ISO 3382 recommends spatial averaging.
• 4 sweeps averaged — Best used for relative before/after comparison.
• Subwoofer contribution — Low-frequency decay includes speaker ringing, not just room modes.

About This Test

This report measures how long bass energy lingers in the room after a tone stops, across the 20–80 Hz range. Four 15-second logarithmic sine sweeps were played through the studio monitors while a measurement microphone recorded the room's response; the recordings were pip-aligned and averaged together to improve low-frequency signal-to-noise. The averaged recording was deconvolved against the known sweep to produce the room impulse response. Frequency-dependent window FFT-based Cumulative Spectral Decay (CSD) analysis provides a time-frequency time-frequency surface, and a per-frequency decay-slope fit (T30, −5 to −35 dB) derives T60 across frequency. The professional monitoring target for T60 is 0.25–0.55 seconds.

Method: FFT-based CSD via the Goertzel algorithm (exact-frequency, no FFT-bin staircase) with a fixed 250 ms window and 5 ms time step. This gives optimal frequency resolution where it matters (low frequencies) and optimal time resolution where it matters (high frequencies). Single mic position; 4-sweep synchronous averaging.
